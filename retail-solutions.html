HOW TO BEGIN

Start with the decision you need to make.

A store manager may need to respond to long checkout queues. An operations team may want consistent visitor counts. A security team may need defined activity alerts. Each objective should have a measurable acceptance test.

  • Define the operational decision and target metric.
  • Confirm the camera view captures the intended area.
  • Test on representative footage, including peak activity.
  • Review model errors and user response to alerts.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

Clear answers before you plan your video analytics project.

Which retailers can use video analytics?

Relevant environments include supermarkets, fashion shops, electronics retailers, department stores and multi-location chains. Feasibility depends on camera coverage and the use case.

Can AI automatically recognise theft?

The described platform detects configured events, not criminal intent. Alerts need human review and must not be presented as conclusive evidence of theft.

Does queue length equal waiting time?

No. Queue length alone does not establish waiting time. A reliable waiting-time estimate requires additional measurement and validation.

What if customers are partly hidden in the video?

Occlusion, lighting and camera placement can reduce detection or tracking quality. These conditions should be included in pilot validation.

Can I compare retail branches?

Yes, if the planned deployment provides multi-store reporting. Use consistent counting definitions and address stream outages when interpreting results.
